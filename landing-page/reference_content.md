## The Hook

AI agents are only as powerful as they are secure — but today, most companies ship them blind, with no real way to stress-test them against real-world attacks. We fix that.

## The Problem

AI agents derive their power from three things: the intelligence of the model, how much data they can access, and how much autonomy they're given to act. That's also exactly what makes them dangerous when compromised.
Unlike traditional software, AI systems are attacked through language — by anyone, not just expert hackers — and the attacks can go undetected for a long time. Existing security tools weren't built for this.

## The solution

Red Sentinel is a crowdsourced AI red teaming platform. Companies deploy their AI systems on our platform as "Sentinels," and a global community of red teamers, anyone from security researchers to curious users tries to break them.
What makes us different is the trust and incentive layer underneath. Every attack is verified using cryptographic attestations generated inside a Trusted Execution Environment, and rewards are processed instantly on-chain via the Sui blockchain. You can't fake a successful attack. You can't delay a payout.
For defenders, it's an always-on, real-world stress test with a public resilience score that doubles as a security audit. For attackers, it's a skill-building game where breaking things pays real money.

The Economics
Attackers pay a small fee per message. That fee splits three ways: half goes back into the reward pool — so the longer a Sentinel holds, the bigger the bounty grows. Forty percent goes back to the defender for putting capital at risk. Ten percent goes to the protocol.
This flywheel means better-defended systems attract more attackers, which generates more data, which helps defenders improve further.

## A few delivery tips

**For general tech investors** who may not know crypto — lead with the AI security problem hard, and frame the blockchain layer as "the mechanism that makes it trustless and scalable," not as the product itself. Don't open with Sui.

**For Web3 investors** — you can lean into the on-chain verification angle and the token incentive design earlier. They'll appreciate the economic flywheel detail.

**The one question you'll always get:** *"Why does this need to be on blockchain?"* Your answer: verifiable proof of attack authenticity that neither party can dispute, and instant trustless settlement — something a centralized platform simply can't offer credibly.
